Three ways to support your child today.

You do not need to be a cybersecurity expert. Consistent conversations and clear family expectations make a meaningful difference.

💬

👀

🤝

Make sure your child knows who to contact, what evidence to save, and when to block or report an account.

What Your Child Is Learning

A family guide to the five Safetii Net missions.

Each mission focuses on one essential digital-safety skill. Use the questions below to continue the learning at home.

1 Identity Protector Personal, private, and secret information +

What children practice

Recognizing which information can be shared safely and which information needs stronger protection.

Try asking

  • What information should never be posted publicly?
  • Who are the trusted adults you can ask before sharing?
  • What could someone learn from a username or profile?
2 Password Safe Keeper Password strength and account protection +

What children practice

Building stronger password habits, avoiding password reuse, and understanding extra account protections.

Try asking

  • Why is reusing one password risky?
  • What makes a passphrase easier to remember but harder to guess?
  • What should you do if someone asks for your password?
3 Phish Finder Suspicious messages, links, and online offers +

What children practice

Slowing down, noticing pressure, checking unusual requests, and avoiding misleading links.

Try asking

  • What makes an online offer feel suspicious?
  • Why do scammers try to make people hurry?
  • How can we check whether a message is trustworthy?
4 Digital Footprint Defender Posts, choices, privacy, and online reputation +

What children practice

Thinking about audiences, screenshots, privacy, permanence, and the effects of online choices.

Try asking

  • Who might see something after it is posted?
  • How could a screenshot change the situation?
  • What should you think about before posting someone else’s picture?
5 Cyber Responder Blocking, reporting, saving evidence, and asking for help +

What children practice

Recognizing when to stop responding, save evidence, block or report an account, and contact a trusted adult.

Try asking

  • What should you save before blocking an account?
  • Which trusted adults could help in different situations?
  • Why is it important not to handle every online problem alone?

When Something Goes Wrong

Use the Safetii Net family response plan.

Children need simple steps they can remember when an online situation feels unsafe, threatening, or confusing.

1

Pause

Stop clicking, replying, sending, or sharing.

2

Save

Keep screenshots, usernames, messages, and other evidence.

3

Block or report

Use the platform tools when it is safe and appropriate.

4

Tell an adult

Bring the situation to someone prepared to help.

💡

Start by listening. A calm response makes it easier for a child to explain what happened and ask for help again in the future.
