Identity Protector

In this mission, you will learn how to protect your personal information online and understand why some details should stay private.

Mission Goal

Your identity is made up of information that tells people who you are. Some information is okay to share, but some information should be protected because it can be used to find you, contact you, pretend to be you, or access your accounts.

By the end of this mission, you should be able to tell the difference between safe information, private information, and risky information.

Key Vocabulary

Personal Information: Details about you, such as your full name, birthday, school, address, phone number, or location.
Private Information: Information that should not be shared online because it can put you or your family at risk.
Oversharing: Posting or telling too much personal information online.

What Counts as Personal Information?

Personal information is anything that can help someone identify you or learn where you are. Some examples may seem harmless by themselves, but when they are put together, they can reveal a lot.

Examples of information to protect:

  • Your full name
  • Your home address
  • Your phone number
  • Your school name
  • Your birthday
  • Your passwords
  • Your current location
  • Your parent or guardian’s information
  • Photos that show your house, street sign, school logo, or license plate

A cyber-safe person thinks before sharing. Even a photo can accidentally share private information if something important is visible in the background.

Safe to Share vs. Not Safe to Share

Usually safer to share:

  • Your favorite color
  • Your favorite book or movie
  • A hobby you enjoy
  • A drawing or project you made
  • A nickname that does not reveal your real identity

Not safe to share:

  • “I live at 123 Main Street.”
  • “I am home alone right now.”
  • “My password is...”
  • “I go to this school every day.”
  • “I am at this exact location right now.”

Real-Life Example

Jayla posts a picture in her school hoodie. In the background, her street sign is visible. She also writes, “Walking home by myself today!”

This post shares more than Jayla may realize. Someone could learn her school, her neighborhood, and that she is alone. Even though she did not post her full address, the details together could create a safety risk.

Better choice:

Jayla could post the picture later, crop out the street sign, avoid sharing that she is alone, and check with a trusted adult before posting.

Mini Activity: Sort the Information

Decide whether each item is safe to share or should stay private.

  1. My favorite animal is a dolphin.
  2. My school is Lincoln Middle School.
  3. My password is MemeRobot123.
  4. I like drawing and soccer.
  5. I am home alone until 6:00.

Cyber-safe thinking means asking: “Could this information help someone find me, contact me, pretend to be me, or guess something private?”

Quick Check

Question 1: Why should you avoid posting your address online?

Question 2: Is your favorite food usually private information?

Question 3: What should you do if you are unsure whether something is safe to post?
