Cyber Responder

In this mission, you will learn what to do when something online feels unsafe, confusing, scary, or uncomfortable.

Mission Goal

Being safe online is not only about preventing problems. It is also about knowing how to respond when something goes wrong.

A Cyber Responder does not panic, hide the problem, or handle everything alone. A Cyber Responder pauses, saves evidence, blocks or reports when needed, and tells a trusted adult.

Key Vocabulary

Cyberbullying: Using technology to hurt, embarrass, threaten, or repeatedly bother someone.
Report: Using a website, app, school, or trusted adult to tell someone about unsafe behavior.
Evidence: Information that shows what happened, such as screenshots, messages, usernames, dates, and times.

When Should You Respond?

You should respond when something online makes you feel unsafe, pressured, threatened, embarrassed, or uncomfortable.

Examples include:

  • Someone asks for your password.
  • Someone sends mean or threatening messages.
  • Someone asks you to keep a secret from your parents or guardians.
  • Someone sends inappropriate content.
  • Someone pretends to be you online.
  • You click a suspicious link by mistake.
  • You see someone else being bullied online.
  • Your account acts strangely or you cannot log in.

The Cyber Responder Steps

1. Pause

Do not reply right away when you are upset or scared. Take a moment to think.

2. Do Not Engage

If someone is bullying, threatening, or trying to trick you, arguing back can sometimes make the situation worse.

3. Save Evidence

Take screenshots, write down usernames, save messages, and note the date and time.

4. Block or Report

Use the app or website’s tools to block the person or report unsafe behavior.

5. Tell a Trusted Adult

A trusted adult can help you decide what to do next. This could be a parent, guardian, teacher, counselor, troop leader, or another safe adult.

What Not to Do

  • Do not keep unsafe messages a secret.
  • Do not delete everything before showing a trusted adult.
  • Do not send your password to anyone.
  • Do not meet someone in person that you only know online.
  • Do not respond with threats or insults.
  • Do not blame yourself for asking for help.

Real-Life Example

Someone sends Mia a message saying, “Send me your password or I will post an embarrassing picture of you.”

Mia should not send her password. She should take a screenshot, avoid arguing, block or report the person, and tell a trusted adult immediately.

Why?

Threats and pressure are serious. Mia does not have to handle it alone.

Mini Activity: What Should You Do?

For each situation, choose the safest response.

  1. You accidentally click a suspicious link.
  2. A stranger asks what school you go to.
  3. A classmate posts a mean comment about someone.
  4. Your game account says someone logged in from another device.
  5. Someone tells you not to tell your parents about your messages.

The safest answer usually includes pausing, not sharing private information, saving evidence, and asking for help.
