Password Safe Keeper

In this mission, you will learn how passwords protect your accounts and how to create stronger passwords that are harder to guess.

Mission Goal

Passwords are like digital keys. They help protect your games, school accounts, email, apps, and personal information.

A weak password is easy for someone to guess. A strong password makes it much harder for someone to get into your account.

Key Vocabulary

Password: A secret word, phrase, or set of characters used to access an account.
Passphrase: A longer password made from multiple words or a sentence.
Two-Factor Authentication: An extra security step that helps prove it is really you logging in.

What Makes a Password Weak?

Weak passwords are easy to guess because they use common words, names, birthdays, or simple patterns.

Weak password examples:

  • password123
  • 12345678
  • qwerty
  • Your name
  • Your pet’s name
  • Your birthday
  • Your school mascot

These are risky because someone who knows a little about you may be able to guess them.

What Makes a Password Strong?

A strong password is longer, harder to guess, and different from your other passwords.

A strong password usually has:

  • At least 12 characters
  • Uppercase and lowercase letters
  • Numbers
  • Symbols
  • No easy personal details
  • No common words by themselves

Better idea: use a passphrase

A passphrase is easier to remember but harder to guess. For example, instead of using one simple word, you can use a sentence or a group of random words.

Example: PurpleRobotDancesAt7!

This is stronger because it is longer, uses different types of characters, and is not just one easy word.

Password Safety Rules

  • Do not share your password with friends.
  • Do not use the same password for every account.
  • Do not write passwords where other people can see them.
  • Do not use personal details that people can guess.
  • Ask a trusted adult before changing important account settings.
  • Use two-factor authentication when available.

Real-Life Example

Marcus uses the same password for his game account and school account. His friend guesses the game password and then tries it on his school account too.

This is why it is risky to reuse passwords. If one account is compromised, the other accounts may be at risk too.

Better choice:

Marcus should use different passwords for important accounts and ask a trusted adult about using a password manager.
