Phish Finder

In this mission, you will learn how to spot suspicious messages, links, and scams before they trick you.

Mission Goal

Phishing is when someone tries to trick you into giving away private information, clicking a dangerous link, downloading something harmful, or sending money.

A Phish Finder does not rush. A Phish Finder stops, checks, and asks for help when something feels suspicious.

Key Vocabulary

Phishing: A trick used to steal information or get someone to click something unsafe.
Scam: A dishonest trick designed to fool someone.
Suspicious Link: A link that may lead to a fake or unsafe website.

How Phishing Tries to Trick You

Phishing messages often try to make you feel rushed, scared, excited, or curious. The goal is to get you to act before you think.

Common tricks include:

  • “Click now or your account will be deleted!”
  • “You won a free prize!”
  • “Your password has expired. Log in here.”
  • “Your package cannot be delivered.”
  • “Send this code to prove it is you.”

Real companies usually do not ask for your password, private codes, or personal information through random messages.

Warning Signs of Phishing

  • The message creates urgency or fear.
  • The sender’s email address looks strange.
  • There are spelling or grammar mistakes.
  • The link does not match the real website.
  • It asks for your password or private information.
  • It promises something that sounds too good to be true.
  • It asks you to keep the message secret.

Real-Life Example

A student gets a message that says: “Your game account will be deleted in 10 minutes! Click this link and enter your password now.”

This message uses fear and urgency. It wants the student to panic and click quickly.

Better choice:

The student should not click the link. They should go directly to the official app or website, check the account there, and ask a trusted adult for help.

The Stop, Check, Ask Method

1. Stop

Do not click right away. Take a breath and slow down.

2. Check

Look at the sender, the link, the spelling, and what the message is asking you to do.

3. Ask

If you are unsure, ask a trusted adult before clicking, replying, or sharing information.

Mini Activity: Spot the Red Flags

Read this message and find the warning signs.

“CONGRADULATIONS!!! You won a free tablet. Click this link in the next 5 minutes and enter your name, address, birthday, and password to claim your prize.”

Look for spelling mistakes, urgency, private information requests, and prizes that sound too good to be true.
